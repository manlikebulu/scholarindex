About

Most scholarship lists waste your time.

Search results are full of dead links, expired deadlines, and pages that ask for a fee before they show you anything. We index a smaller set of awards and check each one against the funder's own website.

We list awards that a named institution or government actually administers. If we cannot find the programme on the funder's domain, it does not go in the index. When a scheme closes, we remove it.

Method

Three rules the index runs on.

  • Sourced from the funder

    Every listing points to the page where the award is administered — a ministry, a university, or a foundation. No intermediate forms. No agent fees.

    See a listing →

  • Structured for filtering

    Each award carries a host country, a funding level, a degree level, and the fields it covers. You filter. We do not guess what you meant.

    Open the search →

  • Rechecked each cycle

    Deadlines move and programmes close. We re-open every link on a rolling schedule and delist anything that no longer resolves to a live programme page.

    Report a broken listing →

    Field notes

    How to read a scholarship listing.

    • What "fully funded" actually covers

      2026

      Tuition, stipend, insurance, and travel are four separate line items. Most awards described as fully funded cover two or three of them. Read the line items.

      Browse fully funded awards →

    • Reading a deadline correctly

      2026

      Many programmes carry two deadlines: one for admission to the university and one for the scholarship itself. Missing the second is the most common failure.

      Browse doctoral awards →

    • Why we delist programmes

      2025

      When a funder pauses a scheme, aggregators often leave the page up for years. We remove the listing the week we confirm the programme has stopped.

      Report a listing →
